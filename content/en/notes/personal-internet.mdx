Social platforms feel like busy streets. They let us meet many people, but they also ask every sentence to prove within seconds that it deserves attention.

A personal website is different. There is no recommendation engine urging me onward and no publishing rhythm that must be filled. It can be slow, opinionated, and willing to keep long notes useful to only a few people.

## A room, not a display case

I do not want this site to exhibit only the “best” work. I would rather preserve the process: why a project began, why a piece of code was rewritten, and what the weather felt like behind a photograph.

The site becomes more like a room. Visitors can see unfinished things on the desk and notice what its owner has been caring about lately.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A principle**
>
> Choose a form for the expression first, then choose tools for that form. Never let a publishing system decide what you are allowed to say.

## Local files are a kind of freedom

In the end, every note is simply an MDX file in a repository. There is no remote database, proprietary editor, or format that disappears with a service. The text can be versioned, moved, searched, and opened in the most ordinary way many years from now.

It may not be the most efficient publishing workflow, but it feels honest—and durable.
